**A** on a game plays it, and one game runs at a time. While it runs, **HOME** on your controller opens the dock over it, and holding **HOME** goes straight back to Universe. The dock needs Universe Big Screen running full screen, which is how it starts. [Where the dock works](/docs/using/playing/#where-the-dock-works) lists the cases where it doesn't.

## Launching

The game's poster, its art with its logo and title, fades in and stays until the game's own window is up. Presses do nothing meanwhile, except **B**, which drops the poster once the game has started and lets it load behind Universe. **HOME** during the wait opens a small dock with **Home** and **Quit** alone, and nothing freezes.

If the game needs a runner Universe hasn't installed yet, such as a Proton build or an emulator, Universe asks first, with **Install and play** and **Not now**. A launch that fails says why, in a message starting "Could not launch".

Playing another game while one runs asks whether to quit the first, with **Quit and start** and **Keep playing**. The new game starts once the old one has closed.

## The dock

<Clip name="home-over-game" />

**HOME** over a running game opens the dock. The game's card at the left shows its art, **PLAYING** or **PAUSED**, and its title. The clock and your controller's battery are at the top right, after **REC** and the time while the session is being recorded. A second **HOME**, or **B**, closes the dock.

The buttons, left to right:

- **Resume** closes the dock.
- **Home** brings Universe up and leaves the game behind it, frozen while Pause on HOME is on.
- **Quit** asks first, since unsaved progress is lost, with **Quit** and **Keep playing**.
- **Game** opens a card whose **Details**, **Journal**, **Recordings** and **Sessions** take you to that page of the game in Universe.
- **Screenshot** takes a screenshot ([Screenshots](/docs/using/playing/#screenshots)).
- **Controllers** lists your controllers, then **Find controllers** ([Controllers](/docs/using/playing/#controllers)).
- **Achievements**, when the game lists achievements, opens them in the tray under the dock.
- **Performance** holds **MangoHud**, **FPS limit**, **Filter**, **Sharpness** (while Filter is on FSR or NIS) and **Pause on HOME**.
- **Sound** holds **Volume** and **Output**.
- **System** shows on a machine Universe can tune, such as a Steam Deck, with **Brightness**, **Refresh rate**, **Power limit**, **GPU clock** and **SteamOS fan curve**.

Left and Right walk the row, or change the focused value; Up and Down move through an open card's rows. **A** acts, flips a switch or opens a card, and **B** closes the card, then the dock. **X** takes a screenshot from anywhere in the dock. A click on the game behind the dock, or a right click, counts as **B**, and the mouse wheel steps a value.

Down from the row raises a tray with the game's screenshots, this session's first. **A** shows one full screen and **Y** sends it to the trash, after a question. When the game lists achievements, **LB** and **RB** switch the tray to them. When a GOG game unlocks an achievement, a banner shows at the top right of the screen; Steam and Epic report theirs after the session.

## HOME

- Pressed over the game, HOME opens the dock. With Universe on screen and a game running, it goes back to the game. With no game running, it opens the [game menu](/docs/using/game-page/#the-game-menu) of the focused game.
- Held over the game for 600 ms, it goes straight to Universe's Home, whatever the press opened. Settings › **Controller**'s advanced **Hold length (ms)** sets the hold, from 400 to 1000.
- With another app in front, HOME brings Universe to the front. **HOME opens Universe**, in the same advanced rows, turns that off.
- No macro can take HOME, and a keyboard has no key for it, so the dock needs a controller.

Back on Home, the cursor lands on the game, whose last frame shrinks into its tile. The tile keeps that frame, the hero's button reads **Resume** and the tab bar shows a badge with the game's title and time. **A** on the tile, the badge or **Resume** grows the frame back to full screen and returns to the game.

## Pause on HOME

With **Pause on HOME** on, as it is by default, the game freezes while Universe covers it. It freezes when the dock opens and when you go Home, and runs again as the dock closes or as you resume. A recording pauses with the game, so menu time never lands in the video. The dock and the home tile read **PAUSED** meanwhile. Nothing freezes while a game is still loading or once you've quit it.

Turn it off for a game that must keep running, online play for one. The game then runs on behind the dock and reads every button you press there.

The dock's switch sets the playing game's own value, and the card under it says it's for this game only. **Y** there gives the value to all your games, except those with a value of their own. The default for every game sits in Settings › [Game defaults](/docs/settings/game-defaults/), and each game's in its **Game settings**.

## Screenshots

The **Screenshots** feature has to be on, and like every feature it's off at first. Turn it on in Settings › **Features**; [Screenshots](/docs/add-ons/screenshots/) has its settings. Without it, the dock answers **Screenshot failed**.

Then take one:

- with **Screenshot** in the dock, or **X** anywhere in it. The dock fades out first, so it stays out of the picture;
- with a controller button set to the Screenshot macro. The Share button of an Xbox pad, the Capture button of a Switch Pro Controller, the left Fn button of a DualSense Edge and the P3 paddle of an Xbox Elite come set that way, and Settings › Controller binds one on any pad ([Macros and paddles](/docs/controllers/macros/));
- with [`universe screenshot`](/docs/reference/cli/screenshot/) in a terminal.

A shutter sounds and the screen flashes white. The file lands in `~/.local/share/universe/games/<id>/screenshots/`, named by the date and time, or in `~/.local/state/universe/screenshots/` when no game is running. Inside Universe's own gamescope the picture holds the game alone, without MangoHud, until you turn off **The game's window alone, not every monitor** in the feature's settings.

## MangoHud, the frame rate limit and scaling

Apart from **Pause on HOME**, the **Performance** card changes the running game alone, and its next launch starts from the game's settings again. Set a value you want to keep in the game's **Game settings**, or for every game in Settings › Game defaults. [Gamescope, HDR and frame rate](/docs/running/gamescope/) explains those settings.

- **MangoHud** shows or hides the overlay with the frame rate, the frame time and the GPU and CPU load. Nothing draws it if MangoHud isn't installed.
- **FPS limit** reads **Auto** (the refresh rate the game sees, and no limit for an emulator, which paces itself), **None**, or a rate, starting at the screen's refresh and going down through the usual rates to 30. MangoHud holds the limit whether its overlay shows or not.
- **Filter** sets how gamescope scales the game, with **Default**, **Linear**, **Nearest**, **FSR**, **NIS** or **Pixel**. FSR and NIS sharpen; Nearest and Pixel keep pixel art crisp.
- **Sharpness**, for FSR and NIS, runs from **0 · sharpest** to **20 · softest**, or **Default**.

The MangoHud macro on a pad toggles it the same way, for this session alone. It comes set on the DualSense Edge's right Fn button, the Xbox Elite's P1 paddle and the 8BitDo Pro 3's R4.

## Volume and output

On **Volume**, Left and Right step the level, 2 % at a time; **Volume step (%)**, in Settings › Controller's advanced rows, changes the step. Right also unmutes, and **A** mutes. Muted, the level stays on screen, dimmed beside a mute sign.

**Output** steps through the sound devices and switches to one once you rest on it for half a second.

A pad's **Volume up**, **Volume down** and **Mute** macros work with the dock closed too, and show the level over the game for a moment. Volume up and Volume down come set on the back buttons of a DualSense Edge, the P2 and P4 paddles of an Xbox Elite, and PR and PL on an 8BitDo Pro 3.

## Controllers

**Controllers** has a row for each controller Universe reads, named after it, with **Bluetooth** or **USB** and its charge when it's known. **A** on a row closes the card and reconnects that pad:

- A Bluetooth pad is disconnected, then connected again, and a message says it's back. One that doesn't answer has switched itself off; press its PS or HOME button to wake it.
- A pad on a cable can't be reconnected from software. The message asks you to pull it out and plug it back in.

Either way, a game that uses [Virtual pads](/docs/add-ons/virtual-pads/) sees its pads unplugged and plugged back in, and each one takes back the player it had.

**Find controllers**, the last row, closes and reopens every pad and tries again the devices Universe passed over, then says how many controllers it found. Use it when a pad in your hands isn't listed. In this look, the two exist only in the dock, and [`universe controller reconnect`](/docs/reference/cli/controller/) reconnects pads from a terminal.

## Quitting

You quit a game with the dock's **Quit**, which asks first, or with **Quit** and the title in the game menu, which doesn't. Quitting Universe, or logging out of the Universe session, closes the game too, and so do the pad's **Stop the game** macro (a hold, bound to no button at first) and [`universe stop`](/docs/reference/cli/stop/).

Universe comes back on screen first, and the game's tile reads **QUITTING**. The game is then asked to close; one that doesn't is asked again after 3 s and stopped 5 s later. Whether it saves on the way out is up to the game. A message follows with how long you played.

When a game crashes, or the system kills it, a message says so, with the time played, and points to its log under **Sessions and logs** on the game's page. A game that exits by itself brings Universe back on its own.

Once a session ends, Video capture files the recording and the Play journal starts its entry, if they're on. With **Back up after playing** on in Settings › Game defaults, the game's saves are backed up.

## Where the dock works

The dock, the screenshot flash and the volume level over the game belong to Universe Big Screen running full screen in its own gamescope, the way it starts by default. Elsewhere:

- **Big Screen on the desktop**, in a window (`--windowed` or `--size`) or because gamescope failed to start, has no dock. HOME brings its window up over the game. A game frozen by Pause on HOME stays frozen until you resume it from Universe, even if you switch to it from the desktop. Screenshots get the shutter sound but no flash.
- **Under [Steam's Game Mode](/docs/getting-started/steam-deck/#steams-game-mode)**, HOME over a game stays Steam's. Steam owns MangoHud, the frame rate limit, the volume, screenshots and power, and the pad's macros for those do nothing.
- **Games started from Universe Desktop or `universe play`** have no overlay of their own, so no dock. The pad's macros still work while they run.
- **An add-on theme** draws no dock. Holding HOME brings the launcher back.

## In the other looks

The PS5 look opens its **Control Center** over the game. Cards along the top show the game (playing or paused, and for how long), its **Game Hub**, its trophies and your captures. Under them runs a bar of icons with **Home**, the game itself (**Resume**, **Game Hub**, **Pause on HOME**, **Close Game**), **Screenshot**, **Controllers**, **Trophies**, **Performance**, **Sound**, **System** and **Power**. □ takes a screenshot from anywhere in it. While the game loads, only **Home** and **Power** show.

The Switch 2 look has no overlay. **HOME** over a game flips to its HOME menu, and **HOME** again returns to the game. On the running game's tile, **A** resumes, **Start** opens its options and **X** closes it, after a question. This look has no MangoHud, frame rate or volume controls of its own, though the settings and the pad's macros still apply. Its **Controllers** page, on the HOME menu, lists each pad with **Reconnect**, then **Find Controllers**.
